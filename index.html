A benchmark score means little without the procedure that produced it.

EvalSpec is a minimum reporting standard for LLM evaluation results. It names the fields a published score has to carry so that a reader can tell what was measured: 13 Required, 5 Recommended and 3 Full. EvalSpec is an extended version of the Every Eval Ever (EEE) schema.

In the registry now: google/gemma-2-9b, google/gemma-7b, allenai/Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B.

What the paper found

We harmonized 46,559 published evaluation records from 12 sources, covering 7,398 models and 1,954 benchmarks, and audited what each record says about how its score was produced. The article goes through the evidence with interactive figures.

Only 0.69% of model and benchmark pairs are reported by more than one source: 291 of 41,999.

The blue sliver is drawn to scale.

Where two sources do report the same pair, 58% differ by more than 5 percentage points.

77 pairs agree within 1 point, 45 differ by 1 to 5, 169 by more than 5.

The fields

Most EvalSpec fields name a place that already exists in an EEE record and make it mandatory. The ones EEE has no slot for, such as scoring_mode, are added. The Spec page gives types, rules, the JSON Schema and worked examples.

Required 13 fields

Lets a reader decide whether two scores were produced under the same regime.

  • model_id
  • benchmark_id
  • scoring_mode
  • harness_name
  • harness_version
  • n_shot
  • benchmark_variant
  • judge_model
  • evaluation_timestamp
  • prompt_template_hash
  • answer_parser_versionwhen scoring_mode is generation
  • judge_prompt_hashwhen a judge produces the score
  • judge_score_rulewhen a judge produces the score

Recommended 5 fields

Adds what is needed to re-run the evaluation.

  • temperature
  • top_p
  • quantization
  • hardware
  • random_seed

Full 3 fields

Adds what is needed to audit the evaluation.

  • provenance_url
  • contamination_check
  • compute_hours

The registry

This build reads the sample bundled with the site: 122 records that the paper and its code repository quote in full. The full corpus of the paper has 46,559 records and lives in the EvalSpec datastore; the Data page says how to build the site from it.

The registry holds 122 records for 20 models and 15 benchmarks, from 17 sources. 64 are author-reported and 58 are predicted. Sources are ordered by how much of the procedure they report themselves, so a source moves up by publishing its own records.

Report a result

Every record goes to the EvalSpec datastore on Hugging Face as a pull request. Records written by whoever ran the evaluation and records filled in by a language model are both welcome, and are shown separately.

You ran the evaluation

A harness holds the full configuration when it computes a score. The released hook for lm-evaluation-harness writes it out as a record when the run ends.

Emit records from a run

You published the result

Fill in the fields for a score in your paper or model card, or open the predicted record for it and correct what a model guessed.

Write or correct a record

You extracted it with a model

Records read from a paper by a language model are uploaded by a person and name the model that extracted them. They are listed as predicted until an author confirms them.

Upload predicted records
