A benchmark score means little without the procedure that produced it.

EvalSpec is a minimum reporting standard for LLM evaluation results. It names the fields a published score has to carry so that a reader can tell what was measured: 13 Required, 5 Recommended and 3 Full. EvalSpec is an extended version of the Every Eval Ever (EEE) schema.

What the paper found

We harmonized 46,559 published evaluation records from 12 sources, covering 7,398 models and 1,954 benchmarks, and audited what each record says about how its score was produced. The article goes through the evidence with interactive figures.

Only 0.69% of model and benchmark pairs are reported by more than one source: 291 of 41,999.

The blue sliver is drawn to scale.

Where two sources do report the same pair, 58% differ by more than 5 percentage points.

77 pairs agree within 1 point, 45 differ by 1 to 5, 169 by more than 5.

The fields

Most EvalSpec fields name a place that already exists in an EEE record and make it mandatory. The ones EEE has no slot for, such as scoring_mode, are added. The Spec page gives types, rules, the JSON Schema and worked examples.
