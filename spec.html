EvalSpec 1.0

EvalSpec is a proposal. It has not been validated as a standard: no record collected from public sources for the paper satisfies the Required tier, and what it costs a harness or a leaderboard to adopt it has not been measured. It applies to results published from now on and cannot repair historical records.

EvalSpec makes protocols visible. It does not make them the same: two fully documented scores produced by different procedures are still not comparable, and the record only lets a reader see that.

Relation to Every Eval Ever

EvalSpec is an extended version of the Every Eval Ever (EEE) schema. EEE stores a model's result on a benchmark, together with its source and its evaluation library, as one JSON file. The records behind the paper use EEE 0.2.1 with eight additional fields: shots, chain_of_thought and reasoning_mode in generation_args; parameter_count, quantization and model_alignment in model_info; dataset_split and sample_size in source_data.

EvalSpec is a conformance profile over that schema, not a competing format.

  • Every EvalSpec record is a valid EEE file. A tool that reads EEE reads an EvalSpec record unchanged.
  • Where EEE has a slot for a field, EvalSpec names that slot and makes it mandatory.
  • Where EEE has none, the field is written as text in generation_config.additional_details of the result it describes. EEE reserves that object for further key and value pairs, so the file stays valid.
  • scoring_mode is the field the paper adds in substance. Base EEE leaves the scoring mode implicit in the metric name at best, as in acc_norm against exact_match.

The schema file the tools check against is eee-0.2.1-extended.schema.json, copied from the paper's code repository.

Fields

The fields come in three cumulative tiers. Where the paper has evidence, the tiers follow it: prompt and shot count moved scores by tens of points and are Required, while temperature barely moved them and is Recommended.

The literal unknown, an empty text and null all mean that a field is not on record. judge_model is the exception: there null states that no judge was involved.

Scoring modes

scoring_mode takes one of four values, set by how the reported number is produced.

log-likelihood
The model assigns a probability to each pre-specified answer option and the highest (length-normalized) token log-probability is selected. No free text is generated.
generation
The model produces free text and the answer is extracted from it by a parser or regular expression before scoring.
preference
A human or model judge rates or ranks outputs and the score is derived from those judgments, for example a win rate, an Elo score or a 1 to 10 rubric rating.
execution
The output is run against test cases or a formal verifier and scored by the result, for example a unit-test pass rate or a proof check.

A pipeline with several stages is labelled by the stage that produces the reported number, and the composite is recorded in benchmark_variant.

  • Ranking candidates by log-likelihood and then generating a rationale is log-likelihood if the ranked selection is scored, and generation if the generated text is scored.
  • A judge applied to a short answer extracted from free text is preference when the rating is the score, and generation when the extracted answer is matched against a key.
  • Chain-of-thought followed by a multiple-choice post-check is labelled by the final selection mechanism.
  • When two stages contribute to one merged score, the primary stage goes in scoring_mode and the secondary stage is named in benchmark_variant.

For human raters, write preference and describe the raters in judge_model, for example human, three raters per item.

Provenance and tracks

A populated field may have been reported by the source, or inferred and supplied by someone else. Every value therefore carries one of five provenance tags.

source-reported
Stated by whoever produced the result.
API-exposed
Returned by the source's own interface.
LLM-extracted
Read from a publication by a language model.
documentation-inferred
Established from platform documentation, not from the record.
pipeline-default
Filled in by a pipeline where the source is silent.

A record states its tag once, for all its values, in source_metadata.additional_details.evidence_source. A single field can differ: evidence_source.n_shot next to it overrides the tag for n_shot. In a flat record, evidence_source is either one tag or an object that maps default and field names to tags.

The tag decides which of two tracks a record is shown in.

Author-reported
The record was written or emitted by whoever ran the evaluation. Its default provenance tag is source-reported.
Predicted
The record was filled in by someone other than the evaluator: read from a paper by a language model, or inferred from documentation and defaults. A person still uploads it.

A record read from a paper by a language model also names the model that read it, in source_metadata.additional_details.extractor_model, in the same way a judged score names its judge.

Records written before the tags existed are read as follows. A file whose source_type is evaluation_run is source-reported. A file extracted from an arXiv paper is LLM-extracted. Anything else is documentation-inferred, except that the names of the model and the benchmark count as API-exposed: a source always states those itself.

Conformance

A record reaches the Required tier when every Required field that applies to it holds a valid value. Recommended and Full are cumulative: Recommended needs all Required and all Recommended fields, Full needs all three.

Ten Required fields apply to every result. answer_parser_version applies when scoring_mode is generation. judge_prompt_hash and judge_score_rule apply when scoring_mode is preference or judge_model names a scorer; a judge name alone does not fix the measurement. judge_model may be left out when the scoring mode is log-likelihood, generation or execution, and is then read as null.

Below the Required tier, a record is described by its coverage: the number of Required fields on record out of the number that apply. The registry's board counts procedure fields only, which are the Required fields other than model_id and benchmark_id, and it counts a field as first-hand when its tag is source-reported or API-exposed.

A record is well formed when it names a model and a benchmark, has a numeric score, and holds no malformed value, such as a hash that is not 64 hexadecimal digits or a harness version of latest. The datastore accepts every well-formed record, whatever its tier.

The validator checks form, not truth. It cannot tell whether a hash covers the system instruction, or whether a shot count is the one that was used.

Check a record in the browser

The same rules are available as one Python file without dependencies: download evalspec.py and fields.json into one folder.

python evalspec.py check my_records/ --verbose
python evalspec.py check record.json --require-tier required

Record formats

The datastore holds EEE records, one per line in a file for each source. A harness, or a person filling in a form, more easily produces a flat record: one JSON object whose keys are the EvalSpec field names. The tools read both, and convert a flat record into an EEE record.

python evalspec.py to-eee flat_records.json --output my_submission/

Score and scale

The score is not among the 21 fields: every EEE record already carries it, with the name and the range of its metric in metric_config. EEE requires min_score and max_score for a continuous score, so 0.585 and 58.5 cannot be confused. A flat record gives them as min_score and max_score.

A score that was rescaled after it was measured should say so in metric_config.evaluation_description, and can keep the measured value in score_details.details.raw_score. This matters: of the 34.1 points between the two published scores for Gemma-2-9B on BBH, 19.7 are the leaderboard's rescaling of 53.77 to 34.10.

Versions and open questions

1.0

The field set of Table 2 of the paper, with the rules needed to check a record by machine. Relative to the examples printed in the paper, this version settles five details.

  • The benchmark is named in benchmark_id. The released hook writes benchmark; the tools accept it and say so.
  • The run time is evaluation_timestamp, not eval_timestamp.
  • Hashes are written in full: sha256: and 64 hexadecimal digits.
  • The score's range travels with the score, so that 0.585 and 58.5 are told apart.
  • Provenance is one tag per record with per-field overrides, in evidence_source.

Open questions

  • EEE paths. The paper fixes the path of judge_model and states that scoring_mode has none. The remaining paths in the tables above are this version's reading and may change.
  • Rescaled scores. Whether a score is raw or rescaled is recorded in free text today. A dedicated field is a candidate for the next version.
  • What a prompt hash covers. The released hook hashes the task template only. A conformant hash also covers the system instruction and the chat template, and the exact canonical form is not yet fixed.
  • Newer EEE versions. EEE has moved on since 0.2.1. The profile will need to be restated over a later version.

Changes are proposed through issues on the code repository. A released version does not change; a revision gets a new number and its own schema file.
